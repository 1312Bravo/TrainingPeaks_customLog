# Evidence And Coaching Sources

These notes track where the training-card ideas come from and how each source is used. The cards should stay practical and concise, but this file keeps the reasoning traceable for later reporting.

## Periodization Structure

### NSCA / Human Kinetics: Hierarchical Structure Of Periodization Cycles

Link: https://www.nsca.com/education/articles/kinetic-select/hierarchical-structure-of-periodization-cycles/

Used for:

- The overall hierarchy of macrocycle, mesocycle, microcycle, training days, and sessions.
- The idea that larger planning phases should be subdivided into smaller, more specific structures.
- The distinction between general preparation, specific preparation, competition, and transition-style phases.

How it maps into cards:

- `MacroCard` represents the larger phase or goal period.
- `MezzoCard` represents focused blocks inside the macro phase.
- `MicroCard` represents weekly organization.
- `SessionCard` represents individual workouts.

### TrainingPeaks: Macrocycle, Mesocycle And Microcycle Guide

Link: https://trainingpeaks.com/blog/macrocycles-mesocycles-and-microcycles-understanding-the-3-cycles-of-periodization/

Used for:

- Practical coaching language around macro, meso, and micro planning.
- The idea that base, build, race-specific preparation, peak/taper, and recovery phases are common practical planning categories.
- The principle that periodization should balance overload, adaptation, and recovery.

How it maps into cards:

- Supports the first compact macro library:
  - `Return To Consistency`
  - `Base Development`
  - `Build Phase`
  - `Race-Specific Preparation`
  - `Peak And Taper`
  - `Recovery And Reset`

## Tapering

### Mujika And Padilla: Scientific Bases For Precompetition Tapering Strategies

Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12840640/

Used for:

- The `Peak And Taper` macro card.
- The idea that tapering should reduce accumulated fatigue while preserving key adaptations.
- The practical principle that volume usually decreases while some intensity is maintained.

How it maps into cards:

- `Peak And Taper` emphasizes freshness, reduced load, maintained rhythm, and avoiding last-minute fitness testing.

## Uphill And Downhill Running Demands

### Vernillo et al.: Biomechanics And Physiology Of Uphill And Downhill Running

Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27501719/

Used for:

- Trail and mountain adaptations inside otherwise general running cards.
- The distinction between uphill metabolic/propulsive demands and downhill mechanical/eccentric demands.

How it maps into cards:

- Supports trail-specific notes about vertical gain, climbing, descending, eccentric load, and terrain-specific fatigue.
- Helps justify why downhill load should progress gradually even when aerobic effort feels controlled.

### Lu et al.: A Review Of Uphill And Downhill Running

Link: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12592170/

Used for:

- Current review-level support for uphill and downhill running as distinct biomechanical and physiological stresses.
- The idea that slope, pacing, cadence, footwear, individual characteristics, and speed can alter load and injury risk.

How it maps into cards:

- Supports keeping card titles broad while adding adaptable terrain guidance inside card details.
- Supports avoiding false precision around exact gradients or terrain formulas.

## Current Card Design Implication

The first macro cards are not meant to be the final complete library. They are a compact starting set based on common periodization categories:

- return / transition into training
- base development
- build development
- race-specific preparation
- peak and taper
- post-race or post-block recovery

More macro cards can be added later if real coaching use shows a distinct phase is missing. The deeper layers should naturally contain more cards than the macro layer because blocks, weeks, and sessions need more variation.

## Strength, Economy, And Durability

### Balsalobre-Fernandez et al.: Strength Training And Running Economy Meta-Analysis

Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26694507/

Used for:

- The `Strength Endurance Block` mezzo card.
- The idea that strength training can support running economy when added thoughtfully to endurance training.
- The caution that strength work should support running quality rather than simply add fatigue.

How it maps into cards:

- Supports controlled strength-endurance work, hill strength, and strength support as complements to running.
- Supports warnings about adding heavy strength or hill load too quickly.

### Blagrove et al.: Strength Training Effects On Running Performance Determinants

Link: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5889786/

Used for:

- Strength-support guidance across build, strength-endurance, and durability-focused cards.
- The distinction between running economy, force-related qualities, and endurance performance determinants.

How it maps into cards:

- Supports including strength and mobility as support tools without making every running card a gym plan.

## Intensity, Threshold, And Aerobic Power

### Garcia-Pinillos et al.: HIIT In Recreational Endurance Runners

Link: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6188912/

Used for:

- The `Aerobic Power Block` mezzo card.
- The idea that high-intensity interval work can improve endurance-running performance when combined with continuous running.
- The caution that high-intensity work is potent and should be placed carefully.

How it maps into cards:

- Supports short aerobic-power blocks, limited weekly key sessions, and strong recovery guidance.

### Wen et al.: HIIT And Moderate Continuous Training Effects On Running Economy

Link: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12946864/

Used for:

- Threshold and aerobic-power card guidance.
- The idea that different intensity methods may affect running economy, lactate-related outcomes, and aerobic qualities differently.

How it maps into cards:

- Supports keeping threshold and aerobic-power blocks separate instead of treating all intensity as one category.
- Supports using controlled intensity rather than making all hard work maximal.

## Fueling And Hydration Practice

### ACSM Position Stand: Nutrition And Athletic Performance

Link: https://acsm.org/education-resources/pronouncements-scientific-communications/position-stands/

Used for:

- The `Fueling Practice Block` mezzo card.
- The idea that nutrition and hydration should be practiced as part of training, especially for longer or more demanding events.

How it maps into cards:

- Supports treating fueling as an execution skill, not only a race-day detail.
- Supports testing intake strategies before goal-specific long runs or race simulations.

## Micro Weeks And Session Design

### Practical Periodization Sources Above

Links:

- https://www.nsca.com/education/articles/kinetic-select/hierarchical-structure-of-periodization-cycles/
- https://trainingpeaks.com/blog/macrocycles-mesocycles-and-microcycles-understanding-the-3-cycles-of-periodization/

Used for:

- Treating micro cards as weekly structures rather than individual workouts.
- Treating session cards as reusable workout patterns that can appear in many different weeks or blocks.

How it maps into cards:

- `MicroCard` examples include recovery weeks, volume progression weeks, intensity support weeks, long-run weeks, race-practice weeks, and taper weeks.
- `SessionCard` examples include easy runs, long runs, threshold intervals, aerobic-power intervals, hill work, strides, race simulation, hiking practice, and downhill conditioning.

### Trail-Specific Session Implications

Links:

- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27501719/
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12592170/

Used for:

- Separating uphill, downhill, and technical-terrain stress from simple pace or distance.
- Writing session cautions around eccentric load, downhill soreness, technical risk, and effort-based pacing.

How it maps into cards:

- `Downhill Conditioning Run` is treated as a controlled mechanical-load session, not just an easy run downhill.
- `Hiking Or Power-Hiking Practice` is treated as a race-execution and efficiency skill for relevant terrain.
- Hill sessions are broad and adaptable rather than tied to exact gradients.
